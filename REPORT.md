# How to write the report

You're reading this because a prompt linked here. "The person" means whoever pasted that prompt into their assistant. The prompt gives the report's question, structure and length; this guide covers the rest.

- **Voice.** Write in your own voice, about the person in the third person by their name, using "they" rather than a gendered pronoun. "I" means only you ("I couldn't open the calendar"). The person's voice appears only in quotes, and section labels are in the third person too ("In their words").
- **Opening.** A few sentences that answer the question. Write it last, from the body.
- **Note.** Two or three lines: what the report covers, which sources you couldn't see, and how you chose what to include. Describe anything left out in general terms only.
- **Numbers.** Use only numbers a reader understands at a glance, each counting one kind of thing. Compute them with code where you can, write "counted by hand" where you can't, and never estimate. Keep them in the overview table rather than scattering them through the text.
- **Excerpts.** Copy every excerpt and quote exactly, with its source and date. If you can't, leave it out rather than reword it. Include both what the person wrote and what the assistant replied where that shows how the work went. Other people's words get at most a sentence or two per excerpt; summarise the rest. Mark anything you infer as inferred.
- State facts without labels like "key" or "strong". Don't describe how you searched.
- **Length.** Keep to the length the prompt gives. Fewer, fuller examples beat many thin ones.
- **Before handing over,** check that everything the opening and the table list has its section, that numbers agree wherever they appear, and that every excerpt is exact.
